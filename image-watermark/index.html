Add a Watermark to Your Image

Stamp your name or © line onto a photo before it travels — position, opacity and size under your control, at full resolution.

Private by design: this tool runs entirely in your browser. Your files are never uploaded to a server, and nothing is stored.

How to use it

Choose a photo. It loads onto a canvas at full resolution — a copy, so your original is untouched.
Style the mark. Type your text, pick a corner (or the centre for proofs), and tune opacity and size while the preview updates live.
Download the PNG. Stamped, full-size, ready to post — the photo never left your device.

Credit that travels with the picture

Photos get re-shared stripped of their captions within hours of posting; the name inside the pixels is the only credit that survives the journey. A watermark also does quiet legal work: it announces ownership to anyone who finds the image out of context, which deters casual reuse and strengthens your position if reuse turns commercial. Doing it in the browser matters more here than anywhere — the whole point is protecting a valuable original, and uploading that original to a stranger's server to protect it would defeat the purpose.

A signature that travels with the photo

A watermark does two jobs at once. It tells everyone who sees the image whose it is — a name, a brand, a handle burned visibly into the picture — and it changes the economics of theft: a clean photo is one right-click from being someone else's listing, portfolio piece or advertisement, while a watermarked one has to be cropped, cloned or explained. Photographers previewing client galleries, sellers whose product shots keep appearing on rival stores, and artists posting work in progress all watermark for the same reason: most image theft is opportunistic, and a visible mark defeats opportunity.

Placement and strength are a craft decision. A small, semi-transparent mark in a corner is polite and easily cropped away; a larger mark across the centre protects completely and ruins the viewing; the working compromise most professionals use is a medium-opacity mark across a lower third or over an area of low detail, visible when you look for it, unobtrusive when you do not. This tool gives you the controls that matter — text, position, size and opacity — with a live preview so the balance is judged on the actual photo, not guessed.

Be honest about the limit: a watermark deters and attributes, it does not make theft impossible — determined editors can remove one, which is why the original unwatermarked file stays your legal proof of ownership. Your photos are processed entirely in your browser and never uploaded, so the only watermarked copies in existence are the ones you choose to download and share.

Questions, answered

Does watermarking reduce my photo's quality?

The watermark is drawn on a copy at the photo's full original resolution — no resizing, ever. You choose the download format: PNG keeps every pixel exactly (lossless, but the file can be several times larger than the original photo), while JPG or WebP at high quality keep the file close to the photo's original size with no visible difference.

What makes a good watermark?

Your name, handle or © line, at 40–60% opacity in a corner: clearly yours when someone looks, unobtrusive when they enjoy the photo. A huge centred mark protects harder but ruins the image for honest viewers too — most photographers keep the strong version for proofs and the corner version for publishing.

Can a watermark be removed?

A determined editor can clone over a corner mark, which is why watermarks are a deterrent and an ownership signal, not a lock. Pair publishing-size images (not full-resolution originals) with a watermark for practical protection.

Is my photo uploaded to be stamped?

No. The photo is drawn onto a canvas on your device; the file never leaves your phone or computer.

More than a party question

"How much older is Dad than Mum?" "Are the twins exactly the same age as their cousin?" Age gaps decide real things too — school year placement between siblings, eligibility windows, and the generation maths behind family trees. Year subtraction alone gets the answer wrong whenever a birthday has not happened yet this year; this calculator borrows months and days properly, so the gap it quotes is the gap that is true. Birth dates are calculated on your device and never sent anywhere.

The age gap, worked exactly

Subtracting birth years gives a rough answer that is wrong for most of the year — it only settles once the younger person's birthday has passed. This age difference calculator uses the full borrowing method instead: full years, then the months left over, then the days, counting every month at its real length, February and leap years included. The result is the gap a school placement, a family tree or a form would recognise. And because it runs in your browser, the birth dates you enter are never sent anywhere.

Worked examples

Two real calculations, so you can see exactly what the tool above does with your dates:

Notice the second pair: the birth years are three apart (2015 and 2018), yet the true gap is under two and a half years — the months and days decide. That is why this calculator counts full years first, then the months left over, then the days, instead of just subtracting years.

Age gaps change meaning across a lifetime

A two-year gap is enormous between a four-year-old and a six-year-old — one is starting school while the other is halfway through it — and almost invisible between a 44-year-old and a 46-year-old. The numbers in this calculator do not change, but their weight does, which is why the same tool serves such different visitors: parents spacing siblings, couples checking a gap they have never actually measured, and families building trees where a "generation" turns out to be 22 years in one branch and 38 in another.

School systems quietly run on this arithmetic. Cohort cut-offs ("must be five by 1 September") mean two children born weeks apart can land a full school year apart, while children born eleven months apart share a classroom. The exact gap in years, months and days — not the rough difference in birth years — is what decides it, and the same precision matters for eligibility windows in sport, where youth leagues are split by birth date, and for benefits and pensions keyed to a spouse's age.

For the family historians: the total-days figure is the one to record. Years and months are how people talk, but days are how records agree — two sources that disagree about a birth date by a few days will show it instantly in the day count, while a years-only comparison hides it completely. As with every calculator here, the birth dates are crunched in your browser and never sent anywhere.

Reading the gap in months — and one more worked example

Years and months are how people talk about a gap, but months alone are often the number a form or a rule actually wants: "at least 18 months apart", "born within two years of each other". The months figure is simple once you have the years: multiply the full years by 12 and add the leftover months. A gap of 4 years and 3 months is 51 months; the worked example below, 3 years and 7 months, is 43 months.

Person 1Person 2Exact age gap
5 January 200023 August 20033 years, 7 months, 18 days (1,326 days apart)

Notice what the year subtraction alone would have said: 2003 − 2000 = 3 years, which happens to match the full years here only because the younger person's birthday (August) had already passed in the comparison year. Had the second birthday fallen in November instead, the true gap would have been 3 years and 10 months — ten months more than the year subtraction suggests. Full dates, borrowed properly, are the only honest count.

Questions, answered

How is the age gap worked out?

We count the full years, months and days between the two birth dates — the same borrowing method used for exact ages — and also show the total days apart.

Does the order matter?

No. Enter the dates in either order; the result tells you which person is older and by how much.

Can I compare a person with something that is not a person?

Any two dates work — siblings, partners, parents and children, even a person and a historical event. Only the dates matter.

What is an age difference calculator?

A tool that works out the exact gap between two birth dates — not just the difference in birth years, but the true gap in years, months and days, plus the total number of days between the two dates.

Roman Numeral Converter

Numbers into Roman numerals, and Roman numerals back into numbers — for years, chapters, outlines, clocks and monuments.

—

How to use it

Type in either box. A number becomes Roman numerals; Roman numerals become a number.
Invalid entries are explained — the rules (no IIII, no VX) are enforced, so what converts is always a proper numeral.
Use it for years — film credits, cornerstones, book chapters and Super Bowl numbers.

Seven symbols do all the work

I1V5X10
L50C100D500
M1000

Numerals are built by adding symbols in descending order — VIII is 5 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 8. The one twist is subtraction: a single smaller symbol placed before a larger one is taken away, which is why 4 is IV (5 − 1) and not IIII. Only six subtractive pairs are standard: IV (4), IX (9), XL (40), XC (90), CD (400) and CM (900). Learn those, and every numeral from 1 to 3999 is just chunks: thousands, hundreds, tens, ones.

Landmarks and famous years: 50 = L, 100 = C, 500 = D, 1000 = M · 1066 = MLXVI (the Norman Conquest) · 1969 = MCMLXIX (the Moon landing) · 1999 = MCMXCIX · 2024 = MMXXIV · 2026 = MMXXVI.

The number system with no zero and a two-thousand-year career

Roman numerals are addition wearing a toga: I, V, X, L, C, D, M stand for 1, 5, 10, 50, 100, 500 and 1,000, and a number is built by stacking them — mostly adding left to right, with the famous subtractive twist that a smaller numeral before a larger one subtracts instead (IV is 4, IX is 9, CM is 900). This converter works in both directions across the classical range 1 to 3,999: type a number and read its numerals, or paste numerals and get the number, with the rules applied strictly so malformed sequences are caught rather than flattered.

The range limit is itself a history lesson: classical Latin had no zero and no symbol beyond M, so 3,999 (MMMCMXCIX) is where the standard system honestly runs out — later conventions drew a bar over a numeral to multiply it by a thousand, but that is an extension, not the original game. What survives everywhere is the usage: film and book copyright years (the BBC's closing credits made MCMXCIX briefly famous), monument and foundation inscriptions, clock faces — which traditionally write 4 as IIII, a clockmaker's exception that has outlived every attempt to correct it — regnal numbers, outlines, and the Super Bowl, whose numbering is now more famous than the system that produced it.

Conversions are computed on your device. Two thousand years on, the numerals are still legible; this page just saves the counting on your fingers.

Questions, answered

What is 2026 in Roman numerals?

MMXXVI — MM for 2000, XX for 20 and VI for 6, added together.

How do I read a year like MCMXCIX?

Work in chunks from the left: M (1000) + CM (900) + XC (90) + IX (9) = 1999. The subtractive pairs — CM, XC, IX — are the ones to learn.

Why is there no Roman numeral for zero?

The Roman system predates the concept of zero as a number — it was built for counting and records, not algebra. Zero arrived in European maths centuries later, via Arabic numerals.

What is the largest standard Roman numeral?

3999 = MMMCMXCIX. M (1000) can repeat at most three times, so without the medieval overline (vinculum) trick, the system stops just short of 4000.

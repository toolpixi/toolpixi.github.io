MP4 to MP3 Converter

Extract the audio from any video as a clean MP3 — converted by your own browser, so the video never leaves your device.

Private by design: this tool runs entirely in your browser. Your files are never uploaded to a server, and nothing is stored.
Tap to choose a video — or drop it here
MP4, WebM, MOV and other common video files

Honest limits: the conversion engine downloads once (about 30 MB) and then runs entirely on your device — powerful, but slower than a server for very long videos, and big files use a lot of memory. Only convert videos you own or have the right to use.

How to use it

Choose your video. MP4 works best; WebM, MOV and MKV are read too.
Pick a quality. 192 kbps suits almost everything; 320 kbps for music.
Convert and download. The audio is extracted on your device and the MP3 downloads straight away.

The audio track, without the upload

A lecture you want to listen to on a run, a song from a live recording, the sound from a screen capture — plenty of jobs need only a video's audio. Server-based converters make you upload the whole video, wait in a queue, and trust a stranger with the file. This converter runs the open-source FFmpeg engine inside your browser instead: the video is read from your device, the audio is extracted there, and only the finished MP3 ever exists — as your download.

Sometimes the video is just a container for sound

A surprising amount of the world's audio travels inside video files: the lecture recorded as MP4, the interview saved from a camera, the podcast published to a video platform, the voice memo a phone insisted on filming. Playing video to hear audio wastes storage, battery and screen — and many places audio needs to go (a car stereo, a podcast app, an editor's timeline, a transcription service) want an audio file, full stop. This tool extracts the soundtrack from an MP4 and hands it back as an MP3, the audio format everything accepts.

The extraction is faithful to the source: the MP3 can only ever sound as good as the audio inside the MP4, because that audio was usually compressed when the video was made. A spoken-word recording — a lecture, a meeting, an interview — comes through essentially perfect for its purpose, and at a fraction of the video's size, which is why a two-hour recorded class can shrink from gigabytes to a file that fits anywhere. Music recorded from a phone at a concert will faithfully reproduce a phone recording at a concert; the tool moves sound, it does not restore it.

Everything happens in your browser: the video is decoded on your device, the audio encoded and downloaded, and the file — which might be a private meeting or an unreleased recording — is never uploaded to a server. The obvious copyright note applies with real force here: extract audio from your own recordings and content you have the right to use.

Questions, answered

How do I convert an MP4 to MP3 without uploading it?

Choose the video above and press Convert. The FFmpeg engine runs inside your browser as WebAssembly, extracts the audio track on your device, and hands you the MP3 as a download. The video is never sent to a server.

Which quality should I choose?

192 kbps is the sweet spot — indistinguishable from the source for most listeners. Choose 320 kbps for music you care about, or 128 kbps when a small file matters more (talking-head videos, lectures, voice notes).

Why does the first conversion take a while to start?

The audio engine (about 30 MB) downloads once from a public CDN and is then cached by your browser. After that first load, conversions start immediately. The conversion itself happens on your device, so long videos take longer than short ones.

Can I convert other video formats, like WebM or MOV?

Yes — the engine reads the common video formats (MP4, WebM, MOV, MKV and more) and always writes an MP3. Very unusual codecs can still fail; the tool tells you honestly if it cannot read a file.

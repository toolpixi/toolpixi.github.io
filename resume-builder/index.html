The download button is where most builders charge you

The free resume builder business model is well known: the design is free, the templates are free, the advice is free — and the PDF costs money, because that is the one moment you cannot walk away from. This builder is built the other way. The PDF is rendered by an open-source library (jsPDF) running in your own browser, from details that never leave your device. There is no server storing your career history, no account to create, and no step at which a price can be attached.

That privacy point is worth more than it first looks. A resume is a dense little dossier — full name, phone, email, employers, dates — and the typical builder keeps a copy of all of it. Here, closing the tab erases everything. If you want a record, the PDF on your own device is the record.

What recruiters actually read

Studies of recruiter behaviour keep landing on the same uncomfortable number: the first look at a resume lasts seconds. What survives that look is structure — a clear name and title, a summary that says what you are, job entries whose first lines carry numbers. Duties describe the job you had; achievements describe the value you added, and only one of those gets interviews. “Responsible for a team” is a duty. “Trained a team of six; error rate fell 30%” is an achievement, and it fits in the same line.

Length discipline matters just as much. One page forces the choices that make a resume sharp: the recent and relevant stay, the distant and generic go. The live A4 preview beside the form exists for exactly this — you can see the page filling up as you type, and decide what earns its space while there is still space to decide in.

Questions, answered

Is this resume builder really free — including the download?

Yes, including the download. Many builders let you design for free and charge at the exact moment you need the file. Here the PDF is generated in your browser, so there is no paywall moment at all: build it, download it, apply.

Do I need to create an account?

No account, no email address, no sign-up of any kind. The form lives in your browser tab; your details are never sent to a server, which also means they cannot be leaked, sold or spammed.

Which template should I choose?

Modern (the coloured header band) suits sales, tech, design and most private-sector roles. Classic (serif, centred) reads as formal and safe for law, finance, academia and government. Minimal suits creative and senior roles where restraint signals confidence. When in doubt, Modern is the most widely accepted.

What should I write in each job's description?

Achievements, not duties — one per line, with numbers wherever you honestly can. “Grew territory sales 24% in a year” beats “Responsible for sales”. Start each line with a strong verb: grew, cut, built, trained, launched, saved. Three to five lines per job is plenty.

How long should my resume be?

One page for under ten years of experience; two pages is acceptable beyond that. Recruiters skim for seconds, not minutes — the preview here is a true A4 page, so what you see is the length they get.

What file do I get, and can I edit it later?

A standard PDF — the format every application portal and email inbox accepts. Your entries stay in the tab while you work; if you close it, come back and re-type, or keep the downloaded PDF as your record. Nothing is stored on our side to come back to.

A resume is read in six seconds before it is read properly

Recruiters do not read resumes; they scan them, and the research on that scan is consistent — a handful of seconds decides whether the full read happens. What the scan looks for is structure it can navigate blind: your name and target role unmissable at the top, the most recent work first, each job reduced to what changed because you were there. That is why this builder is built around a clean, conventional layout rather than a decorative one: the design's job is to disappear, leaving the reader alone with the evidence.

The content rules that survive every industry are equally plain. Achievements beat duties — "kept the accounts" is a duty, "closed the monthly accounts two days early" is evidence. Numbers are the strongest words on the page: money handled, people served, time saved, anything countable. One page for early careers, two at the most for senior ones, and every line earning its place against the question "does this make them call me?"

The builder runs in your browser: you fill the sections, watch the document assemble, and download a proper PDF — free, with no sign-up and no account holding your work history hostage. That privacy is not a footnote. A resume is your entire career in one file; on this page it is written, built and downloaded without ever leaving your device.

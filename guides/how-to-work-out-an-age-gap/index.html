How to Work Out an Age Gap Between Two People

The true gap between two birthdays is rarely the difference between two birth years.

The two-birthday problem

"How much older is she than her brother?" sounds like a subtraction, and the subtraction everyone reaches for — birth year minus birth year — is wrong for most of the calendar. Two people born in 1990 and 1992 can be barely a year apart (December 1990 and January 1992) or nearly three years apart (January 1990 and December 1992). Birth months and days decide which, and they decide real things: school cohorts, sibling spacing, eligibility windows, and the quiet arithmetic behind family trees.

The exact method

Work the gap the way you work an exact age: line up the two full birth dates and subtract with borrowing. Full years first, then the months left over, then the days, borrowing across real month lengths as you go. Example: 12 March 1990 and 25 June 1994. Years: 1994 − 1990 = 4. Months: June comes after March, so 3 full months. Days: 25 − 12 = 13. Gap: 4 years, 3 months, 13 days — 1,566 days in total. When the later date's month or day is the smaller one, the borrowing walks the other way, which is where hand calculation traditionally drops a month. The Age Difference Calculator does the borrowing both ways, tells you which person is older, and shows each person's current age alongside the gap.

Where gaps actually decide things

School placement is the heavyweight: systems that admit children by a cut-off date ("five by 1 September") mean an eleven-month gap can share a classroom while a two-week gap across the cut-off separates children by a full year. Youth sports leagues split by birth date for the same reason. In family history, the gap is a checking tool: a claimed mother-child gap of twelve years or a sibling gap of four months flags a record that needs a second look, while the total-days figure exposes two sources disagreeing by days that a years-only comparison hides.

Couples, generations and curiosity

For couples the exact gap is usually pure curiosity — studies put the average gap between married partners at roughly two to three years — but curiosity deserves an exact answer too. Generations are the same maths at family scale: measure the gap between a grandparent's and a grandchild's birth dates and divide by the generations between them, and you have your family's true generation length, which is rarely the round 25 or 30 years people assume. Both birth dates are crunched in your browser and never sent anywhere — family arithmetic stays in the family.

Reading a family tree with gaps

Genealogists use age gaps as a lie detector, and the tests are simple arithmetic. Work down a tree measuring the gap between each parent's birth and each child's. Gaps in the twenties and thirties are the ordinary rhythm of most families across most of history. A gap under about fifteen years between a mother and a child asks for a second look at the records — someone has been attached to the wrong generation, a common error when names repeat. A gap over sixty between a father and a child is possible but rare enough to double-check. Sibling gaps tell stories too: a run of two-year gaps interrupted by a nine-year gap often marks a remarriage, a war, or a child who died young and was never spoken of; twins, of course, show a gap of zero days — or one, when the second twin arrives after midnight, a detail families treasure and records garble.

The generation length ties it together. Take a grandparent born in 1931 and a grandchild born in 1998: the gap is 67 years across two generations, a generation length of about 33.5 years. Measure several lines of the same family and the average is a real fact about that family's history — families that married late run long generations and compress more history into fewer names, which is why one person's great-grandparent can be another's contemporary. All of it falls out of two dates and the borrowing method; the calculator at the top of this page exists so the arithmetic is never the part that goes wrong.

Questions people ask

Can an age gap be negative, or zero? The gap itself is a distance, so it is never negative — the tool reports which person is older instead of using a sign. Zero happens exactly once in nature: twins born minutes apart share a gap of 0 years, 0 months and a handful of minutes, which the day-level gap honestly reports as 0 days.

What is the biggest age gap ever recorded between siblings? Verified cases exceed 40 years — a parent having children across four decades. It is rare enough that a gap like that in a family record deserves a source check, which is precisely the genealogist's test described above.

Does the order I enter the dates matter? No. Older-first or younger-first, the gap and the "who is older" answer come out identical — the borrowing simply runs in whichever direction the dates demand.

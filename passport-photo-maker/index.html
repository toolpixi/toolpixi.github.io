How to use it

Choose a photo — front-facing, head centred, plain light background, even daylight.
Pick the size your form asks for — 35×45 mm, 2×2 in or 51×51 mm — and JPG or PNG.
Press Make my passport photo. The centre crop is resized to the exact pixels that size means at 300 dpi — check the preview, then download.

The specification is the whole job

A passport photo is not judged on how good you look — it is judged on measurements. The face must fill a set share of the frame, the background must be plain white or off-white, the head must be straight and unshadowed, and the file must be exactly the size the form asks for. The photo a form accepts is a specification, not a portrait

A passport photo is not judged on how good you look — it is judged on measurements. The face must fill a set share of the frame, the background must be plain white or off-white, the head must be straight and unshadowed, and the file must be exactly the size the form asks for: 35 × 45 mm in much of Europe and India, 2 × 2 inches in the United States, 51 × 51 mm for an Indian passport. A beautiful photo at the wrong size is rejected as surely as a blurry one, and a photo at the right size but the wrong shape is rejected too. The specification is the whole job.

This tool does the mechanical half of that specification: it takes any photo, crops it from the centre to the exact ratio of the size you picked, and resizes it to the exact pixel dimensions that size means at 300 dpi — 413 × 531 pixels for 35 × 45 mm, 600 × 600 for both square sizes. It does not do the other half, and it says so plainly: it cannot check where your head sits in the frame, whether the lighting is even, or whether you are smiling (most authorities require a neutral expression). Frame your face in the centre of the shot, against a plain light wall, in even daylight, and the crop will land where the rules expect it.

Two practical notes. First, if your photo has a transparent background — a cut-out from another tool — tick the white-background box, or the transparency becomes black in a JPG. Second, always check the current official specification for the document you are applying for: sizes are stable, but rules on glasses, head coverings and photo age change, and the issuing authority's page is the only source that counts. For the file-size side of the same forms, the Image Compressor shrinks the finished photo to the exact KB cap a portal demands.

Questions, answered

What size is a passport photo?

It depends on the country: 35 × 45 mm in much of Europe and for Indian visas, 2 × 2 inches in the United States, and 51 × 51 mm for an Indian passport. Pick your size above — the tool crops and resizes to exactly that size at 300 dpi.

What pixel size will I get?

35 × 45 mm becomes 413 × 531 pixels, and both square sizes (2 × 2 in and 51 × 51 mm) become 600 × 600 pixels — the exact dimensions those print sizes mean at 300 dpi.

Does it check my face position?

No — this tool crops to the exact size; it does not detect faces. Frame your face in the centre of the shot, against a plain light wall, in even daylight, with a neutral expression, and the centre crop will land where the rules expect it.

Should I download JPG or PNG?

JPG for almost every form — it is smaller and every portal accepts it. PNG only if you need the white background kept as a separate layer, which forms do not ask for. JPG is the default here.

Why counting days on a calendar goes wrong

Adding days to a date sounds like arithmetic, but it is really geography: you are travelling across a map of months that refuse to be the same size. January gives you 31 days, February gives you 28 — 29 in a leap year — and April only 30. A count of "90 days from 15 November" crosses three month boundaries and a year boundary, and every crossing is a chance to land a day early or a day late. That one day is rarely harmless. A visa overstayed by a day, a contract notice served a day late, a warranty claimed a day after expiry — the rules written as "within N days" are enforced to the day, not to the nearest week.

The weekday matters almost as much as the date itself. Deadlines that land on a Saturday or Sunday often roll to the next working day in one office and expire on the spot in another, which is why this calculator always shows the weekday next to the landing date: seeing "Sunday, 14 March" in the result is the prompt to check the rule before you rely on it. And when the rule is written in working days rather than calendar days, the honest tool for the job is the Business Days Calculator, which skips weekends as it counts.

One more distinction saves endless confusion: this calculator counts calendar days the way laws, visas and contracts count them — every day on the calendar, weekends included. If a result ever looks one day off, the usual cause is inclusive counting (counting both the first and the last day). The standard here excludes the start day and counts each day after it, which matches how "90 days from today" is meant everywhere from passport offices to probation terms.

Two habits make these counts reliable. First, never convert months to days by multiplying by 30: six calendar months from 17 November 2026 is 17 May 2027, which is 181 days, not 180 — the months in between decide, and a 30-day month is the exception, not the rule. Second, always read the weekday next to the landing date before you rely on it: a deadline that lands on a Saturday is a different problem from one that lands on a Tuesday, and this calculator shows the weekday precisely so you spot it before you commit. Questions, answered

How do I find the date 90 days from today?

Leave the starting date as today, pick 'Add days', type 90, and the answer appears with its weekday — the way visa, probation and countdown questions are usually asked.

Can I count backwards?

Yes — pick 'Subtract days'. 'What date was 45 days ago?' is how people work out deadlines, refund windows and notice periods.

Does it handle weekends or holidays?

This calculator counts calendar days, including weekends — the standard meaning of 'in 90 days'. It does not skip public holidays, which differ by country.

How many days are between two dates?

That is the two-date version of the question, and it has its own tool: Days Between Dates takes a start date and an end date and counts the exact days between them. This date calculator answers the other form — the date a set number of days before or after a given day.

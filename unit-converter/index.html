Built on the exact definitions

Most conversions here are not approximations: since 1959 the inch is defined as exactly 2.54 cm and the pound as exactly 0.45359237 kg, so your answers match official tables. Temperature uses the standard formulas. Volume uses the US liquid gallon — defined as exactly 231 cubic inches, or 3.785411784 litres — and the US customary cup of 236.5882365 millilitres. Whether you are buying fabric in metres for an American pattern, checking a suitcase against a kilogram limit, or reading a Fahrenheit forecast, the number you get is the number that is true.

Two systems, one world, and the cost of confusing them

The world measures in two dialects — metric and imperial — and the border between them runs through kitchens, workshops, gyms and airports rather than along any map. This converter translates across four everyday categories — length, weight, temperature and distance — in both directions, instantly. Its conversions are built on definitions, not approximations: since 1959 the inch has been exactly 2.54 centimetres by international agreement, the pound exactly 0.45359237 kilograms, the mile exactly 1,609.344 metres — so a conversion done here is not an estimate but an identity, the same value any standards laboratory on earth would give you.

Precision has limits worth knowing, and temperature is the famous one: it is the only common conversion with an offset as well as a scale, which is why °C to °F is a formula rather than a ratio, and why doubling a Celsius temperature does not double the Fahrenheit one. Unit mix-ups are also the rare comedy with real casualties — aircraft have run out of fuel and a Mars orbiter was lost to pound-versus-newton confusion — which is the serious argument beneath the everyday one: convert deliberately, with the units named, whenever money, safety or machinery is attached to the number.

For the conversions people make daily, the dedicated pages alongside go deeper — kilograms to pounds, centimetres to inches, the temperature pair, each with its own table and worked examples. This page is the generalist: every unit in those four categories, both directions, computed on your device with nothing stored.

Three habits keep these conversions honest. First, temperature is the only category with an offset as well as a scale — doubling a Celsius figure does not double the Fahrenheit one, so use the formula (°F = °C × 9/5 + 32) rather than a ratio. Second, heights are two numbers, not one: convert feet and inches to total inches first (5 feet 7 inches is 67 inches), then convert the inches — reading 5′7″ as 5.7 feet is the classic error. Third, round only at the end: the converter keeps full precision internally, and the table above shows two decimals, which is plenty for luggage, recipes and road signs.

Questions, answered

How many cm are in an inch?

Exactly 2.54 cm — it is the international definition of the inch, so this converter's answer matches everywhere.

How do I convert kg to pounds?

Choose Weight above (or type here): 1 kg = 2.20462 lb. A 70 kg person weighs about 154 lb.

Is the temperature conversion exact?

Yes. °F = °C × 9/5 + 32, and Kelvin is included for science work.

How many inches are in a foot?

12 inches — and one foot is exactly 30.48 cm. Choose Length above to convert feet, inches, centimetres and metres in either direction.

How to use it

Enter the date of birth.
Enter the date that matters — an admission cutoff, exam date, or any day you are checking.
Get the exact age on that date, in years, months and days, with totals.

The cutoff-date question, answered exactly

"Must be 5 years old on or before 1 September." "Under 19 on the tournament date." School admissions, exam boards, sports federations and pension rules all fix eligibility to a single date, and being a few days out changes the answer. This tool does the fiddly month-and-day borrowing for you and gives an answer you can quote with confidence — calculated privately, in your browser. Comparing two children's ages against each other for a cutoff? The Age Difference Calculator takes two birth dates and returns the exact gap between them.

The eligibility question: "how old will they be on…?"

Most real age questions are not about today. They are about a fixed date in the future with a rule attached to it: will my daughter be five by the school entry cut-off? Will I be 18 by election day, 21 by the flight, 60 by the retirement date, 16 by the exam? The answer decides applications, bookings and plans, and it cannot be worked out from today's age alone — you need the age on that specific date, with the birthday's position in the year taken into account. That is the whole job of this calculator: pick any date, past or future, and read the exact age on it.

The past direction earns its keep too. "How old was I when we moved house?" "How old was Grandad in this 1962 photograph?" Family history, timelines for memoirs and legal statements, and simple curiosity all run on ages at past dates, and the borrowing method — full years, then months, then days across real month lengths — is identical in both directions.

One practical warning: cut-off rules are usually written to the day and enforced to the day, and a child or applicant who misses the date by 24 hours is treated exactly like one who misses it by a month. If the answer here lands within a few days of a cut-off that matters, double-check the rule's exact wording — "by 1 September", "on or before 31 August" and "in the September of the year they turn five" are three different rules wearing similar clothes.

Questions, answered

What is this used for?

Eligibility cutoffs: school admissions ('age on 1 September'), exam age limits, sports age groups, retirement and legal-age checks all ask for your age on one fixed date, not today.

Can the date be in the future?

Yes. Pick any date — past or future — and the calculator gives the exact age on that day.

How exact is it?

It counts full years, then months, then days, matching how ages are officially stated, and shows total days too.

How is this different from an age calculator?

An age calculator measures your age today. This one measures it on any date you choose — backwards ('how old was I when...?') or forwards ('will I be 18 by...?').

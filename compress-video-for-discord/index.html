Compress Video for Discord

Shrink a video to fit Discord's upload limit — re-encoded by your own browser, so the video never leaves your device.

Private by design: this tool runs entirely in your browser. Your files are never uploaded to a server, and nothing is stored.
Tap to choose a video — or drop it here
MP4, WebM, MOV and other common video files

Honest limits: the engine downloads once (about 30 MB) and re-encodes on your device, so long videos take real time and memory. The Discord preset aims for about 10 MB — the exact result depends on the video's length and detail, and very long videos can land over the target. Only compress videos you own or have the right to use.

Why Discord says no: the limits in plain numbers

Discord's upload limit is per file, and it is the number every failed send runs into: "Your files are too powerful" is Discord's way of saying the clip is bigger than your account is allowed to send. The limits, as of November 2026, are 20MB for a free account, 50MB with Nitro Basic ($2.99 a month) and 500MB with full Nitro ($9.99 a month). A server's Boost level lifts the ceiling for everyone posting in that server — Level 2 to 50MB, Level 3 to 100MB — which is why the same clip sends fine in one server and bounces in another.

The free number has moved more than most people realise: 8MB for years, then 25MB in April 2023, then back down to 10MB in September 2024 when Discord cited storage costs, and then up to 20MB in August 2026 — double the old cap. Some free accounts also see 25MB right now as part of an ongoing Discord test, but 20MB is the documented figure, and the history says to treat any limit as current as of this month rather than forever. The practical rule is simple: if the people you are sending to are on free accounts, the file has to fit 20MB — and this page aims at about 10MB, so it fits with room to spare.

What the Discord preset actually does

Pick a video and the tool reads its length, then works out the video bitrate that fits the whole clip into about 10MB: the 10MB budget divided by the seconds, minus the 96 kbps kept for the audio track. A short clip gets a generous bitrate and keeps nearly all of its quality; a long clip gets a thin one, because the same 10MB has to stretch further. The picture is capped at 1280 pixels wide — wider than any phone screen needs for a chat video — encoded with x264 at a fast preset, and the audio stays at 96 kbps AAC, because butchered sound annoys viewers more than slightly softer pictures. The result is an MP4 with fast-start enabled, which is the format Discord handles best.

Two honest boundaries. First, the preset aims for about 10MB: a very noisy or very detailed video — confetti, rain, fast gameplay — costs more bits per second than a calm talking-head clip, so the same length can land a little over the target. Second, there is a floor: below 80 kbps of video the picture falls apart, so the tool will not go lower, which means videos past about eight minutes cannot reach 10MB at all and come out larger. If the video's length cannot be read at all, the tool falls back to the Balanced preset instead of guessing. When the result is still too big, the answer is not a stronger squeeze — it is a shorter video, which is the next section's job.

How long a video fits in 10MB

The maths is the whole story: 10MB spread over the clip's length, minus the audio. These are the video bitrates the preset works out for common lengths, so you can judge before you compress whether the result will look good.

15 secondsabout 5,500 kbpsPlenty — looks close to the original, even at 1080p.
30 secondsabout 2,700 kbpsComfortable — the classic Discord clip, sharp on any screen.
1 minuteabout 1,300 kbpsGood — fine on phones and laptops, slight softening in busy scenes.
2 minutesabout 600 kbpsWatchable — faces and gameplay read clearly, fine detail softens.
3 minutesabout 370 kbpsSoft — fine for a voiceover or a simple screen recording, rough for action.
5 minutesabout 180 kbpsVery soft — only worth it if the content matters more than the picture.

Past about eight minutes the preset hits its minimum bitrate and the file lands over 10MB anyway — that is the point where compression stops being the answer and trimming, splitting or linking takes over. The same table is why a one-minute clip is the sweet spot for Discord: short enough to stay sharp, long enough to say something.

When compressing is not enough: trim, split, or link it

Trim it. Most clips sent to Discord have dead air at the start, a loading screen, or a long tail after the moment that mattered. Cut those with your phone's built-in editor before compressing — a 45-second clip trimmed to 25 seconds nearly doubles its bitrate at the same 10MB, which is a bigger quality win than any encoder setting.

Split it. A three-minute gameplay moment does not have to be one file. Two 90-second parts each compress far better than one three-minute file, and Discord plays them in sequence in the same message. It is also how streamers have always shared long moments in chat — in parts, not in one heroic upload.

Link it. If the video is genuinely long — a full match, a tutorial, a recorded call — upload it to YouTube as unlisted and paste the link. Discord embeds it inline, everyone can watch it in full quality, and the 20MB limit never enters the conversation. Compression is for clips; links are for videos.

Or pay the $2.99. Nitro Basic at $2.99 a month raises your own cap to 50MB everywhere you post, which covers most clips without compression at all. It is worth doing the maths honestly: if you send long videos every day, the subscription is cheaper than the time spent squeezing them — but if it is one clip a week, this page is free, and free wins.

Five habits that make Discord videos play well

Send MP4. Discord's native tongue is MP4 with H.264 video — the format this tool outputs — so the clip plays inline without conversion delays. MOV and WebM usually work too, but they are the files most likely to sit on "processing" while everyone waits.

Keep the frame rate. The preset keeps your video's original frame rate, and that is the right call: a 60 fps gameplay clip dropped to 30 fps looks choppier than a slightly softer 60 fps one, and Discord re-encodes on its side anyway.

Do not strip the audio. A silent clip reads as broken in a chat, and the 96 kbps the preset keeps for audio costs almost nothing against the video budget. If the clip genuinely has no sound worth keeping, the file still benefits — but check before you mute it.

Test it on yourself first. Send the compressed file to your own DM or a private channel and watch it there before dropping it in the group chat. Ten seconds of checking catches the over-compressed clip, the wrong trim and the file that is still 200KB over the limit.

Keep the original. The compressed copy is a sending copy, made for one chat — the original stays on your device at full quality, ready to compress differently if the next destination has a different limit.

If you have Nitro, the Balanced preset keeps more quality — but check the downloaded size against your 50MB or 500MB ceiling before sending, because a long video can still come out larger than the cap.

Questions, answered

How do I compress a video for Discord?

Choose your video above — the Discord preset is already selected — then press Compress Video. The tool measures the video's length and re-encodes it at the bitrate that lands the file at about 10MB, safely under Discord's 20MB free limit, then downloads the compressed MP4. Very long or noisy videos can land slightly over the target; trimming the video first helps.

What is Discord's file size limit?

As of November 2026, a free account can upload files up to 20MB — Discord raised the cap from 10MB in August 2026. Nitro Basic ($2.99 a month) raises it to 50MB, and full Nitro ($9.99 a month) to 500MB. A server's Boost level lifts the limit for everyone in that server: Level 2 to 50MB, Level 3 to 100MB. Some free accounts see 25MB in an ongoing Discord test, but 20MB is the documented number to plan around.

Will my video still look good at 10MB?

For a short clip, yes — a 15–30 second video at 10MB keeps a high bitrate and looks close to the original on a phone or laptop screen. The longer the video, the thinner the bitrate is spread: a one-minute clip still looks good, a three-minute clip is watchable, and past about five minutes the picture softens noticeably. Watch the downloaded file before you send it — if it looks too soft, trim the clip and compress again.

How long can a video be and still fit in 10MB?

Roughly eight minutes at the very most — and comfortably under three. The preset divides the 10MB budget by the video's length, so a 30-second clip gets about 2,700 kbps of video, a one-minute clip about 1,300 kbps, and a three-minute clip about 370 kbps. Past about eight minutes the tool hits its minimum bitrate and the file lands over 10MB anyway, so long videos need trimming or splitting first.

Metres into the yards that sport and fabric still speak

Metres measure the world — the 100-metre sprint, the room that is 5 metres long, the fabric sold by the metre in every country except the United States — and yards are what American sport and craft still think in. The conversion is exact in both directions: the yard is defined as 0.9144 metres, so one metre is 1 ÷ 0.9144 = 1.093613 yards. Ten metres is 10.94 yards, a hundred metres is 109.36 yards, and a kilometre is 1,093.61 yards — just over ten American football fields laid end to end.

Track and field is where the two units meet most visibly, and the comparison is a classic. The 100-metre sprint is 109.36 yards — about nine yards longer than a football field, and 9.14 metres longer than the old 100-yard dash it replaced. The 100-yard dash, the classic American sprint, is 91.44 metres; the world record for it, 9.21 seconds, is a time nobody runs any more because the event itself has faded. The metric distances won: the Olympics run 100 m, 200 m and 400 m, and the yard survives in the sports that never left America.

The metre itself is the more modern definition of the two: since 1983 it has been defined as the distance light travels in a vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second, a definition tied to a constant of nature rather than a bar of metal in a vault. The yard is defined from the metre, not the other way round — which is why the conversion on this page is exact, and why the metres-to-yards direction is the one a metric reader needs when an American pattern, field or fairway quotes yards. Questions, answered

How do I convert meters to yards?

Divide the metres by 0.9144 — or multiply by 1.093613, which is the same thing. So 10 m × 1.093613 = 10.936 yards.

How many yards is 100 meters?

109.361 yards — the 100 m sprint is about 9 yards longer than an American football field (100 yards).

Is a meter longer than a yard?

Yes — one metre is 1.0936 yards, about 3.4 inches longer than a yard. A yard is 0.9144 m.

What is a yard in meters, exactly?

0.9144 m, by the 1959 international agreement — so the conversion is exact, not approximate, in both directions.

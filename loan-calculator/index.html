Loan Calculator — Monthly Payment & Total Interest

Enter the amount, the yearly interest rate and the years — get the true monthly payment and what the loan really costs you in total.

—

Total interest you pay—
Total cost (amount + interest)—
This calculator covers the loan itself — principal and interest on a standard monthly-repayment loan. It does not include arrangement fees, insurance or taxes, and it is a guide for comparing offers, not financial advice.

How to use it

Type the amount you want to borrow.
Add the yearly interest rate from the offer (the APR-style figure).
Set the years and read the monthly payment plus the total interest — the number lenders rarely headline.

The two numbers every borrower should know

The monthly payment tells you whether a loan fits your budget; the total interest tells you what it truly costs. Stretching a loan over more years lowers the monthly figure but quietly raises the total — doubling the term rarely halves the payment but often doubles the interest. Run the same amount at two different terms here and the trade-off stops being abstract. The maths is the standard amortisation formula, run on your device; nothing you type is sent anywhere.

What a monthly payment is really made of

Every loan payment is two payments fused: a slice of the amount you borrowed, and rent on the money still outstanding — the interest. Early in a loan the balance is large, so interest takes the bigger share of each instalment and the debt itself shrinks slowly; as the balance falls, the same fixed payment bites deeper into the principal. That is why the first year of a mortgage feels like standing still, and why the totals in this calculator — total paid, total interest — are the numbers that tell the truth about a loan. The monthly figure tells you whether you can carry it; the totals tell you what it costs.

The term is the lever with the longest shadow. Stretch a loan from 3 years to 5 and the monthly payment drops kindly — while the total interest climbs steeply, because the balance spends two extra years being rented. Halving the term rarely doubles the payment, yet it can cut the interest by half or more. Run any real loan offer through both terms here before signing: the comparison takes ten seconds and is worth more than any negotiation over the rate's second decimal.

This calculator assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments (the EMI pattern). If your offer has a variable rate, fees, or a balloon payment at the end, treat the result as the honest baseline and ask the lender what their additions do to it. The figures you type stay in your browser — loan shopping is another calculation nobody needs to watch you do.

Questions, answered

How is the monthly payment calculated?

With the standard loan formula: the amount, the monthly interest rate (yearly rate ÷ 12) and the number of monthly payments. It is the same maths banks and EMI calculators use, so the payment shown matches a real amortising loan.

What is EMI?

Equated Monthly Installment — the fixed amount you pay every month until the loan ends. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones mostly principal. The monthly figure here is the EMI.

Does this include fees or insurance?

No — it covers the loan itself (principal + interest). Bank fees, insurance and taxes are separate and vary by lender, so add them on top when comparing offers.

What does EMI mean?

EMI — equated monthly installment — is the fixed monthly payment that repays a loan plus its interest over the chosen term. It is the first figure this calculator shows.
